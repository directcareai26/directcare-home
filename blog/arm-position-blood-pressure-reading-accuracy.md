## Arm Position Can Change Your Blood Pressure Reading

 In a 2024 Johns Hopkins trial, resting the arm in the lap overstated blood pressure by about 4 points, and letting it hang at the side overstated the top number by 6.5.

 In the 2024 ARMS trial of 133 adults, a blood pressure reading taken with the hand resting in the lap was about 4 mm Hg higher on both numbers than one taken with the arm supported on a desk. With the arm hanging at the side, the top number read 6.5 mm Hg higher and the bottom number 4.4 mm Hg higher.

 Guidelines say blood pressure should be measured with the arm supported and the cuff at the level of the heart. In practice, readings are often taken with the arm resting in the lap or hanging beside an exam table. Researchers at Johns Hopkins measured how much that changes the result.

#### What the trial did

 The ARMS trial enrolled 133 adults aged 18 to 80 in Baltimore. Each person had sets of three readings taken in three positions, in random order:

- Arm supported on a desk, the recommended position

- Hand resting in the lap

- Arm hanging unsupported at the side

 A fourth set, again on the desk, let the researchers account for normal variation between readings.

#### What it found

 Compared with the desk position:

- Lap: the top number (systolic) read 3.9 mm Hg higher and the bottom number (diastolic) 4.0 mm Hg higher

- Side: systolic read 6.5 mm Hg higher and diastolic 4.4 mm Hg higher

 The pattern held across age groups, body sizes and people with and without high blood pressure.

#### Why a few points matter

 In US guidelines, stage 1 high blood pressure starts at 130 over 80. A person whose true reading is 124 could be recorded at 130 or above because of where the arm was. The authors noted that the error could lead to people being told they have high blood pressure when they do not.

#### How to take a reading you can trust

- Avoid caffeine, exercise and smoking for 30 minutes beforehand

- Empty your bladder, then sit quietly for five minutes

- Sit in a chair with your back supported, feet flat and legs uncrossed

- Rest your arm on a table so the middle of the cuff is level with your heart

- Place the cuff on bare skin, and use a cuff that fits your arm

- Stay silent during the reading

- Take two readings a minute apart and write both down

 At the clinic

 If your arm is left hanging or resting in your lap, it is reasonable to ask for it to be supported on a table or armrest.

#### When to talk with a clinician

 One high reading is not a diagnosis. If home readings are regularly at or above 130 over 80, bring your log to a licensed clinician. Seek emergency care for a reading above 180 over 120 with chest pain, shortness of breath, vision changes or weakness.
