## Coffee Timing: Morning Coffee and the 6-Hour Caffeine Cutoff

 Morning coffee drinkers had a 16% lower risk of death than non-drinkers in a 2025 study, and caffeine taken 6 hours before bed still disrupted sleep in a lab trial.

 In a 2025 study of US adults, people who drank coffee mainly in the morning had a 16% lower risk of death from any cause and a 31% lower risk of cardiovascular death than non-drinkers; all-day drinkers did not. In a separate sleep lab trial, caffeine taken even 6 hours before bedtime significantly disrupted sleep.

 Most coffee research asks how much people drink. Two studies looked at a different question: when.

#### Morning drinkers and all-day drinkers

 Researchers used national survey data to sort US adults by when they drank coffee. Two patterns stood out. About 36% drank it mainly in the morning, and about 14% drank it through the day. Participants were followed for a median of 9.8 years.

 Compared with people who drank no coffee:

- Morning drinkers had a 16% lower risk of death from any cause

- Morning drinkers had a 31% lower risk of death from cardiovascular disease

- All-day drinkers had no significant reduction in risk

 The results were adjusted for the amount of coffee, hours of sleep and other factors. Among morning drinkers, more coffee was linked with lower risk. Among all-day drinkers, it was not.

#### Caffeine six hours before bed

 In a sleep laboratory trial, healthy adults took a moderate dose of caffeine at bedtime, three hours before bed, or six hours before bed, and each condition was compared with a placebo. All three significantly disrupted sleep. The authors concluded that caffeine taken six hours before bedtime has important disruptive effects, and they recommended avoiding substantial caffeine for at least six hours before bed.

 Keep it in proportion

 The coffee timing study is observational. People who drink coffee only in the morning may differ from all-day drinkers in other ways, and the study cannot show that timing causes the difference. The sleep trial was small and short.

#### How to use this

- Have your coffee in the morning

- Set a cutoff at least six hours before bed. For a 10 p.m. bedtime, that is 4 p.m. at the latest

- Count all sources: tea, cola, energy drinks, chocolate and some pain relievers

- If you sleep poorly, try an earlier cutoff for two weeks before changing anything else

#### When to talk with a clinician

 People who are pregnant, or who have heart rhythm problems, anxiety or reflux, are often advised to limit caffeine. Ask a licensed clinician what amount is right for you.
