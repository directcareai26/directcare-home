## Late Eating: Hungrier and Burning Fewer Calories on the Same Food

 In a controlled crossover trial, eating identical meals later in the day increased hunger, lowered daytime energy expenditure and shifted fat tissue toward storage.

 In a 2022 randomized crossover trial in adults with overweight or obesity, eating the same meals on a later schedule increased hunger, raised the ratio of the hunger hormone ghrelin to the fullness hormone leptin, and decreased waking energy expenditure, compared with an early schedule. Nutrient intake, activity, sleep and light were controlled.

 Population studies have linked late eating with higher body weight, but they could not say why. Researchers at Brigham and Women's Hospital designed a laboratory trial to isolate the effect of timing.

#### What the trial did

 Adults with overweight or obesity completed two laboratory stays. In one they ate on an early schedule, and in the other the same meals were shifted later in the day. The trial controlled what and how much they ate, their physical activity, their sleep and their light exposure, so that timing was the only difference.

#### What it found

 On the late schedule:

- Hunger increased

- Appetite hormones shifted toward hunger: the ratio of ghrelin to leptin was higher across the waking day and over 24 hours

- Energy expenditure during waking hours was lower

- Core body temperature over 24 hours was lower

- Gene activity in fat tissue changed in a direction consistent with less fat breakdown and more fat storage

 Keep it in proportion

 This was a small, short, tightly controlled study. It shows how the body responded over days in a lab. It did not measure weight change over months in daily life.

#### Why timing could matter

 The body's internal clock prepares it to handle food during the day. Eating late moves meals toward a time when the body is winding down, which appears to change both appetite signals and how much energy is used.

#### How to try it

- Eat a real breakfast and lunch so that less of the day's food falls in the evening

- Finish dinner a few hours before bed

- If you snack at night, check whether dinner had enough protein and fiber

- Keep meal times fairly consistent from day to day

 Order within a meal matters too. See [vegetables and protein before carbs](https://www.directcare.ai/blog/food-order-vegetables-protein-before-carbs-blood-sugar).

#### When to talk with a clinician

 Shift workers and people with diabetes who take medication should get individual advice before changing meal timing.
