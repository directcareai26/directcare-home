## Protein at Every Meal: What Even Distribution Does for Muscle

 In a 2014 study, the same daily protein split evenly across three meals raised 24-hour muscle protein synthesis 25% compared with loading it at dinner.

 In a small 2014 crossover study, healthy adults who ate about 30 grams of protein at each of three meals had a 24-hour muscle protein synthesis rate 25% higher than when they ate the same total with most of it at dinner. The study was small and measured a short-term marker, not muscle gained.

 Most adults eat protein in a lopsided pattern: a little at breakfast, a moderate amount at lunch, and the bulk of it at dinner. A study from the University of Texas Medical Branch asked whether that pattern matters when the daily total stays the same.

#### What the study did

 Eight healthy adults, average age about 37, followed two diets for seven days each, with a 30-day break between them. Both diets had the same calories and the same total protein, roughly 90 grams a day.

- Even pattern: about 30 grams of protein at breakfast, lunch and dinner

- Skewed pattern: about 10 grams at breakfast, 16 at lunch and 63 at dinner

 Researchers measured how fast the body built new muscle protein over 24 hours, using muscle biopsies and a labeled amino acid.

#### What it found

 Muscle protein synthesis over 24 hours was 25% higher on the even pattern than on the skewed one. The difference was still there after a week on each diet, so it was not a first-day effect.

 Keep it in proportion

 This was a study of eight people, and it measured the rate of muscle protein building, not muscle gained over months. It is a reason to try an even pattern, not proof that it builds more muscle.

#### Why the pattern could matter

 The body does not keep a reserve of protein the way it stores fat or carbohydrate. Each meal with enough protein switches on muscle building for a few hours. A breakfast with 10 grams may not reach the amount needed to do that, and a 60-gram dinner provides more than one meal can use for that purpose.

#### What an even day looks like

 Moving protein from dinner to breakfast is the simplest change. Around 25 to 30 grams looks like:

- Three eggs with a cup of Greek yogurt

- A cup of cottage cheese with fruit and nuts

- A smoothie with protein powder and milk or soy milk

- Leftover chicken, fish or tofu from dinner

 Protein needs vary with age, body size, activity and health. People with kidney disease are often advised to limit protein, so talk with a licensed clinician before raising your intake.
