## The Protein + Fiber Breakfast Template: 3 Fast Rotations

 A breakfast that holds you until lunch needs at least 30g of protein and 8g of fiber. This template gives you three rotations that hit both in under 10 minutes.

 If your breakfast leaves you hunting for a snack by 10:30, the problem is almost always the same: not enough protein, and almost no fiber. The fix is a template, not a recipe — hit the numbers, rotate the ingredients, and the rest takes care of itself.

 Below is the target we build around, the three rotations we actually use, and the small tweaks that make each one work for GLP-1 users, perimenopausal appetite shifts, or a training block.

### Why 30g protein and 8g fiber? the numbers that matter

 Thirty grams is the protein threshold most often cited for meaningful muscle protein synthesis in adults, particularly as we age past 40 and anabolic resistance sets in (Moore et al., J Gerontol, 2015). It's also the dose that reliably blunts the post-meal ghrelin rebound that drives mid-morning snacking.

 Eight grams of fiber is roughly a third of the daily 25–35g target, and — more practically — it's the dose that noticeably slows gastric emptying and flattens the glucose curve after breakfast. Most Americans get about 15g of fiber a day, which is why energy crashes at 10 a.m. feel normal. They aren't.

 A breakfast that hits both numbers is doing two jobs at once: preserving lean mass and stabilizing appetite for the next four hours.
 {callout: The template} Protein anchor (30g+) + fiber source (8g+) + optional fat and flavor. Everything else — sweet, savory, hot, cold — is a rotation, not a rule.

### Rotation 1: Greek yogurt bowl — the two-minute default

 This is the fastest option and the one we recommend when mornings are genuinely chaotic. No cooking, no cleanup.

 **Ingredients**

- 1 cup (200g) nonfat Greek yogurt, 0% or 2%

- 1/2 cup raspberries or blackberries (highest-fiber berries)

- 2 tbsp chia seeds

- 1 tbsp sliced almonds

- Optional: 1 tsp honey or a few drops of vanilla extract

 **Method** 1. Spoon yogurt into a bowl. 2. Top with berries. 3. Sprinkle chia and almonds over the top. 4. Add honey or vanilla if using. 5. Eat.

 **Approximate nutrition:** 32g protein · 12g fiber · 22g carbs · 11g fat · 320 kcal

 **Variations**

- Savory version: swap berries for cucumber, cherry tomatoes, olive oil, and za'atar. Skip the chia; add 2 tbsp hemp seeds.

- Higher calorie (training days): add 1/2 cup rolled oats soaked overnight in the yogurt. Adds ~150 kcal and 4g fiber.

- Dairy-free: use unsweetened soy yogurt (the only plant yogurt with real protein — 8–10g per cup) and add a scoop of unflavored protein isolate to hit 30g.

### Rotation 2: Savory scramble with beans — when you need something hot

 The egg-only scramble is a protein-and-fiber trap: three eggs give you ~18g protein and zero fiber. Beans fix both problems.

 **Ingredients**

- 2 whole eggs + 3 egg whites (or 2 eggs + 1/2 cup liquid whites)

- 1/2 cup canned black beans, rinsed

- 1 cup baby spinach

- 2 tbsp salsa

- 1/4 avocado

- Salt, pepper, olive oil spray

 **Method** 1. Heat a nonstick pan over medium, spray with olive oil. 2. Add spinach, wilt for 30 seconds, then add beans to warm through. 3. Pour in beaten eggs and whites, stir gently until just set (about 90 seconds). 4. Slide onto a plate, top with salsa and sliced avocado. 5. Serve.

 **Approximate nutrition:** 34g protein · 9g fiber · 18g carbs · 14g fat · 340 kcal

 **Variations**

- Burrito bowl style: add 1/3 cup cooked quinoa for +4g fiber and +100 kcal.

- Higher fiber: swap black beans for 1/2 cup edamame — bumps protein another 4g.

- Meal prep: make four portions Sunday night in muffin tins at 350°F for 20 minutes; reheat 60 seconds.

### Rotation 3: Overnight oats, done right — the make-ahead option

 Most overnight oats recipes are dessert with a health halo — 40g of carbs, 8g of protein, and a spike-and-crash by 10 a.m. This version inverts the ratio.

 **Ingredients**

- 1/3 cup rolled oats (not instant)

- 1 scoop (about 25g protein) unflavored or vanilla whey or plant protein

- 1 tbsp chia seeds

- 1 tbsp ground flaxseed

- 3/4 cup unsweetened almond milk

- 1/4 cup Greek yogurt

- 1/2 cup blueberries or diced apple

- Cinnamon to taste

 **Method** 1. In a jar, whisk protein powder into almond milk until smooth (this order matters — protein clumps if added last). 2. Stir in oats, chia, flax, and cinnamon. 3. Top with Greek yogurt, then fruit. 4. Seal and refrigerate at least 6 hours or overnight. 5. Eat cold, or microwave 60 seconds.

 **Approximate nutrition:** 35g protein · 11g fiber · 32g carbs · 9g fat · 380 kcal

 **Variations**

- Chocolate: use chocolate protein, add 1 tsp cocoa powder and a few dark chocolate chips.

- PB&J: 1 tbsp powdered peanut butter mixed in, strawberries on top.

- Lower carb: cut oats to 2 tbsp, double the chia. Drops carbs to ~18g while keeping fiber high.

### What if you're on a GLP-1? the appetite-first tweak

 On tirzepatide or semaglutide, early satiety is the constraint — you can't finish a 400-kcal bowl, and forcing it triggers nausea. The template still works; the portions shrink.

 Aim for the same ratio (protein-forward, fiber-included) in a smaller footprint: 3/4 cup Greek yogurt instead of a full cup, half the scramble, half the oats. What you can't afford to skip is the protein — GLP-1 weight loss is roughly 25–40% lean mass without adequate intake (Wilding et al., STEP 1, NEJM, 2021), and breakfast is the meal most likely to get skipped.

 If a bowl feels heavy, a protein shake with a tablespoon of chia stirred in hits both numbers in 8 ounces.

### The mistakes that quietly break the template

 **Fruit juice instead of whole fruit.** Juice loses the fiber. A glass of orange juice adds 25g of sugar and 0g of fiber to an otherwise good breakfast.

 **"Protein" cereals and bars.** Most deliver 10–15g of protein and count on you not reading the label. If you're using a bar, it needs 20g+ protein and 5g+ fiber to earn the spot.

 **Nut butter as the protein source.** Two tablespoons of almond butter is 7g of protein and 200 kcal — a fat source, not a protein source. Use it for flavor, not for the anchor.

 **Skipping breakfast "because intermittent fasting."** Fine if it works for you, but the research on time-restricted eating for body composition is mixed at best (Lowe et al., JAMA Intern Med, 2020), and skipping breakfast tends to push protein intake below the 1.6 g/kg threshold that supports lean mass in adults over 40.

### How this fits a bigger plan — the labs worth watching

 Breakfast is one lever. If you're using nutrition to move a specific number — fasting glucose, HbA1c, LDL, ApoB, or hs-CRP — the labs worth having before you start optimizing are the ones that tell you which direction to push. A metabolic panel with lipids, HbA1c, and a fasting insulin will show whether the protein-and-fiber template is doing what you want it to do over 8–12 weeks.

 Rotate the three above for two weeks. Then look at how you feel at 11 a.m. — that's the first, cheapest data point you have.
