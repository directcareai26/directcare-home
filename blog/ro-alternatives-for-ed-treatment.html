Why this matters

Erectile dysfunction is a symptom, not a diagnosis you can settle by choosing a website. Blood vessel health, diabetes, medication effects, nerve problems, hormones, anxiety, and relationship stress can all contribute.

The useful question is not only which medication a company offers. It is which care setting can assess your situation, prescribe safely when appropriate, and investigate what an initial prescription does not resolve.

For your 2026 comparison, separate the provider from the treatment. Switching platforms does not necessarily change how a medication works, and staying online does not replace an examination when one is needed.

2. In-person primary care: best for checking the bigger picture

This route is particularly useful when you have not had a general health assessment or cannot explain why symptoms started. It also gives you somewhere to bring concerns that do not fit neatly into an ED intake form.

Where primary care shines

  • A clinician can examine you and measure blood pressure during the visit.
  • Medication review can identify possible contributors without assuming you need another prescription.
  • Your clinician can decide which tests or referrals answer a specific clinical question.

Where primary care falls short

  • Not every primary care clinician provides advanced ED treatment.
  • You still need a referral when symptoms require specialist investigation.
  • An office appointment requires travel and coordination that online care avoids.

3. In-person urology: best for complex or persistent ED

A urologist treats conditions involving the urinary tract and male reproductive system. This is the more relevant alternative when your concern is no longer simply how to access an initial online evaluation.

Penile pain, new curvature, prior pelvic surgery, injury, or treatment that has not worked are reasons to discuss specialist assessment. A urologist can determine whether further investigation or a different treatment approach is appropriate.

Where urology shines

  • Specialist assessment can address anatomical concerns that an online conversation cannot examine.
  • Treatment discussions can extend beyond oral medication when clinically appropriate.
  • Persistent symptoms can be evaluated without assuming that a different website is the answer.

Where urology falls short

  • Specialist care is not necessary for every uncomplicated ED presentation.
  • An in-person visit adds travel and appointment coordination.
  • A specialist visit does not guarantee a particular diagnosis or successful treatment.

4. Qualified sex therapy: best for anxiety-related sexual difficulties

Sex therapy addresses psychological, behavioral, and relationship contributors to sexual difficulties. It can complement medical evaluation when anxiety, fear of losing an erection, or relationship tension is part of the pattern.

It is not evidence that your symptoms are imaginary. Physical and psychological contributors can exist together.

Where sex therapy shines

  • Addresses performance anxiety and patterns of avoidance.
  • Creates space to work on communication and sexual expectations.
  • Can complement clinician-prescribed treatment.

Where sex therapy falls short

  • Does not replace medical assessment for new or persistent ED.
  • Does not investigate vascular, neurological, or anatomical causes.
  • Prescribing depends on the professional's separate medical qualifications, not the sex-therapy label.

Best for: Anxiety or relationship factors that interfere with sexual function, alongside appropriate medical care.

Unlike Ro's medical evaluation pathway, sex therapy focuses on psychological and relationship work. Check the professional's license, training, and scope before booking.

Verdict: Buy into qualified therapy when anxiety or relationship patterns need their own treatment.

Why people switch from Ro

The clinically useful reasons to consider switching are about care fit. They are not evidence of a problem with Ro's service.

  • You need an examination. Pain, curvature, injury, or other physical findings call for an appropriate in-person assessment.
  • You need a wider medical review. ED alongside other health changes deserves more than an isolated medication discussion.
  • Initial treatment has not helped. The next step is to review the diagnosis, instructions, response, and alternatives with a clinician.
  • Anxiety needs direct attention. Medical treatment alone does not address every psychological or relationship contributor.

Before changing providers in 2026, assemble your treatment history. Record the medication name, prescribed dose, instructions, response, and side effects so the next clinician can make an informed decision.

Compare the treatment, not just the platform

Sildenafil and tadalafil are prescription medicines used for ED. Both are PDE5 inhibitors: they support the blood-flow response involved in an erection, but sexual stimulation is still needed.

Sildenafil is commonly taken about 30–60 minutes before sexual activity. Tadalafil can support erectile response for up to 36 hours after a dose; that does not mean a continuous erection. These timing details come from the medicines' prescribing information, not a promise about any provider's service.

Your clinician's directions take priority. Food, other medicines, health conditions, and the prescribed regimen can affect how you should use treatment.

A safer decision sequence

  • Medical history: Share heart conditions, blood pressure concerns, symptoms, and your full medication list.
  • Treatment review: Explain what you used, how you took it, and what happened.
  • Examination needs: Ask whether an office assessment is necessary before changing treatment.
  • Follow-up plan: Establish what to do if treatment fails or causes side effects.

Do not combine ED medication with nitrates or recreational nitrites, including poppers. The combination can cause a dangerous fall in blood pressure. Tell your clinician about every medicine and substance you use.

An erection lasting more than 4 hours needs emergency medical care. If chest pain occurs after ED medication, seek emergency help and tell responders what you took and when; do not self-administer a nitrate.

FAQ

What's the best Ro alternative for ED treatment in 2026?

The best alternative depends on the care you need. DirectCare AI offers sexual health telehealth within a broader treatment scope; an in-person urologist is a better fit when ED requires specialist examination or investigation.

Is another telehealth provider better than Ro?

Another telehealth provider is not automatically better than Ro. Compare clinical scope, medication review, follow-up, and how the provider handles symptoms requiring an examination.

Can I get ED treatment without an office visit?

Some people can receive ED evaluation and prescribed treatment through telehealth. A clinician must decide whether remote care is appropriate or an in-person assessment is needed.

Should I see a primary care doctor or a urologist for ED?

Primary care is a useful starting point for a general health assessment, while urology addresses specialist concerns. Pain, curvature, relevant injury or surgery, and unsuccessful initial treatment are reasons to discuss urology referral.

Does switching providers make sildenafil work better?

Switching providers does not itself change sildenafil's pharmacology. A clinician should review how you took it, your response, other medicines, and whether a different approach is appropriate.

Can low testosterone cause erectile dysfunction?

Low testosterone can contribute to sexual symptoms, but ED alone does not establish testosterone deficiency. Diagnosis requires clinical assessment and appropriate testing; hormone treatment is not a general substitute for ED evaluation.

Can therapy help if I lose erections because of anxiety?

Qualified sex therapy can address performance anxiety and relationship contributors to erection difficulties. Medical evaluation remains important because physical and psychological causes can overlap.

One last thing

An erection problem is not automatically a testosterone problem. Before pursuing hormone treatment or switching ED prescriptions, ask what evidence supports the suspected cause and what finding would change the plan. That question matters as much as which treatment you choose.
