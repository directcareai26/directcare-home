### What a sound TRT evaluation includes

 A useful comparison of providers starts with the clinical process. In 2026, a polished website still cannot replace a diagnosis supported by symptoms and appropriate testing.

#### Symptoms

 Explain what changed and how it affects you. Reduced libido, fewer spontaneous erections, and other symptoms deserve assessment, but none establishes testosterone deficiency on its own.

#### Morning testing

 The American Urological Association recommends diagnosing low testosterone only after **2 total-testosterone measurements on separate early mornings**, together with symptoms or signs. Testosterone varies, so a single result is not enough.

 The association uses **below 300 ng/dL** as a reasonable total-testosterone cutoff supporting the diagnosis. That threshold is not permission to self-prescribe; your clinician must interpret the result in context.

#### Cause assessment

 Ask what the results suggest about the source of the problem. Depending on the findings, clinicians use additional testing, including luteinizing hormone, to help distinguish different causes.

 Also disclose fertility goals, sleep apnea, medications, and relevant medical history. These details affect the decision, not just the paperwork.

#### Monitoring

 Ask how the clinician will review symptoms, testosterone levels, and blood counts if treatment begins. The Endocrine Society's testosterone-therapy guidance identifies a **hematocrit above 54%** as a reason to stop treatment temporarily and evaluate contributing factors under medical supervision.

 Hematocrit describes how much of your blood consists of red blood cells. Do not change a prescribed dose yourself after reading a threshold online.
