How to compare clinical fit before switching

A useful 2026 comparison starts with your health record, not a list of drug names. Bring the information a new prescriber needs to understand both your starting point and your experience so far.

Medical history

Share your diagnoses, prescription medicines, supplements, previous weight-loss treatment, and any treatment-related symptoms. Include pregnancy plans and relevant personal or family medical history. Do not assume a new clinician can see records held by another provider.

Describe symptoms plainly. Persistent vomiting, abdominal pain, constipation, dizziness, or difficulty eating should be discussed as symptoms—not treated as proof that a medication is working.

Treatment eligibility

For adults, FDA prescribing information for Wegovy and Zepbound includes weight-management eligibility at a BMI of 30 kg/m² or greater, or 27 kg/m² or greater with at least one weight-related condition. These thresholds help define an indication; they do not replace an individual assessment.

For adult BMI calculations, divide weight in kilograms by height in meters squared. For example, 1.70 meters is entered as height—not 170 centimeters. A clinician should interpret the result alongside your health history rather than treating the calculation as an automatic prescription.

Follow-up plan

Ask who reviews your response, how you report side effects, and what happens if treatment stops. Establish which questions go to the prescribing clinician and which require urgent or in-person care.

Do not copy your previous dose into a new treatment plan yourself. The new prescriber needs the exact medication, dose, last administration date, and any interruption before deciding what happens next.

Compare the medication—not just the platform

A provider's name does not tell you exactly what will be prescribed. Ask for the medication's full name and whether the prescription is for an FDA-approved product or a compounded preparation.

FDA approval applies to specific products and indications. Compounded medications are not FDA-approved, and the FDA does not review them for safety, effectiveness, and quality before marketing. Do not treat a shared ingredient name as proof that two preparations are equivalent.

For semaglutide and tirzepatide, discuss relevant contraindications and warnings with the prescriber. FDA-approved weight-management products containing these ingredients carry warnings concerning thyroid C-cell tumors and contraindications involving a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or multiple endocrine neoplasia syndrome type 2.

Your clinician also needs to review pregnancy, other medications, and symptoms that affect treatment safety. This article cannot determine whether either ingredient is appropriate for you.

Before agreeing to a prescription, get plain-English answers to these questions:

  • What exact product or preparation are you prescribing?
  • Why does it fit my medical history and treatment goals?
  • What symptoms mean I should stop and seek advice?
  • Who decides whether the dose changes?
  • What should I do after a missed dose or treatment interruption?

Those answers matter more than a provider promising access to a particular ingredient.

FAQ

What's the best Ro alternative for weight loss in 2026?

DirectCare AI is a fit for patients seeking weight-loss telehealth alongside access to hormone care and blood lab testing. In-person obesity medicine care is the better fit when a physical examination is necessary. A clinician must determine whether medication is appropriate.

Is an in-person weight-loss clinician better than telehealth?

In-person care is better suited to a situation that requires a hands-on examination. Telehealth is a different care setting, not proof of lower or higher clinical quality. Choose according to the assessment you need.

Can I keep taking the same dose when I switch from Ro?

Your receiving prescriber must decide whether to continue or change your dose. Share the exact medication, current dose, last administration date, side effects, and any treatment interruption before following new instructions.

Do all weight-loss providers prescribe the same medication?

No, a provider name does not establish the exact prescription you will receive. Ask for the full product or preparation name and whether it is FDA-approved or compounded. A shared ingredient does not establish equivalence.

What BMI qualifies an adult for weight-loss medication?

FDA weight-management indications for Wegovy and Zepbound include adults with a BMI of at least 30 kg/m², or at least 27 kg/m² with a weight-related condition. Meeting a threshold does not guarantee that a medication is appropriate or will be prescribed.

Should I switch providers if weight-loss treatment causes side effects?

Contact your prescribing clinician about side effects before making a medication change. A new provider still needs to assess the symptoms and treatment history. Severe or urgent symptoms require prompt medical evaluation, not a routine provider comparison.

Does access to hormone care mean I need HRT or TRT?

No, access to HRT or TRT does not establish a medical need for either treatment. A clinician must assess symptoms, history, and relevant testing. Do not assume a hormone treatment belongs in a weight-loss plan.

One last thing

Ask one question before you switch: Who owns the next clinical decision? You should know who reviews symptoms, authorizes dose changes, and explains whether treatment should continue.

If the answer is unclear, resolve it before transferring care. A recognizable medication name is not a substitute for a clinician responsible for your treatment.
