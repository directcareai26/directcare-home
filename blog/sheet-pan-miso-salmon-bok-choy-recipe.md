## Sheet-pan miso-glazed salmon with bok choy: a 25-minute, 35g-protein dinner

 Sweet, salty, umami-glazed salmon with charred bok choy and brown rice. The 25-minute dinner that tastes like the restaurant version.

 Recipe for sheet-pan miso-glazed salmon with charred bok choy and brown rice: 25 minutes, 35g protein, omega-3 target hit in one pan.

 Miso-glazed salmon is one of those restaurant dishes that looks complicated and isn't. The glaze is four ingredients and a stir. The salmon and bok choy share a sheet pan. The whole thing is on the table in 25 minutes.

 Pattern adapted from the classic Nobu-style miso black cod, which [Bon Appétit covered](https://www.bonappetit.com/) and home cooks have iterated on for years. We swapped cod for salmon (more accessible, better omega-3 profile) and tightened the marinade time.

### Why this dinner, specifically.

- Salmon brings 30+ g of protein and ~1,500 mg of EPA + DHA per 5 oz fillet. The same omega-3 logic we covered in [the original Mediterranean salmon bowl](https://www.directcare.ai/blog/glp1-mediterranean-salmon-bowl-recipe/).

- Bok choy is a cruciferous vegetable with serious calcium content (~150 mg per cup cooked). Particularly relevant during the [perimenopausal calcium window](https://www.directcare.ai/blog/perimenopause-nutrition-foods-that-help/).

- Miso paste is a fermented soy product. The phytoestrogen content is modest but real, and the umami it adds means the salmon doesn't need a heavy sauce on top.

- Brown rice brings 4 g of fiber per cup cooked. Skip if you want lower carbs.

### Ingredients (serves 2)

 **For the salmon:**

- 10 oz salmon fillets (two 5 oz portions, skin-on)

- 3 tbsp white or yellow miso paste (shiro miso. Find it in the refrigerated aisle near the tofu)

- 2 tbsp mirin (sweet rice wine) or 1 tbsp honey + 1 tbsp water

- 1 tbsp low-sodium soy sauce or tamari

- 1 tbsp rice vinegar

- 1 tsp grated fresh ginger

- 1 garlic clove , grated

- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil

 **For the bok choy:**

- 3 to 4 small bok choy heads (or 1 large), quartered lengthwise

- 1 tbsp avocado or olive oil

- Pinch of salt and pepper

- 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds

 **To serve:**

- 1 cup uncooked brown rice (yields ~3 cups cooked. Extra for leftovers)

- Sliced scallions and extra sesame seeds for garnish

- Lime wedges

### Method (25 minutes start to finish)

#### 1. Start the rice first (25 min cook, but mostly hands-off).

 Bring 2 cups water + 1 cup rinsed brown rice + pinch of salt to a boil. Cover, reduce to low, simmer 22 to 25 minutes until water is absorbed. Let it rest covered while you cook the rest.

#### 2. Make the miso glaze (3 min).

 Whisk together miso paste, mirin, soy sauce, rice vinegar, ginger, garlic, and sesame oil in a small bowl. It should be the consistency of thick honey. Add 1 tsp of water if it's too thick.

#### 3. Marinate the salmon (5 min, optional).

 Pat the salmon fillets dry. Place them on a parchment-lined sheet pan, skin-side down. Spread half the miso glaze over the tops and sides. Let it sit at room temperature 5 minutes while you preheat the broiler.

 Save the remaining glaze for the bok choy and serving.

#### 4. Broil (10 min total).

 Set your broiler on high and position the rack 6 inches from the heat.

 Toss the bok choy quarters with avocado oil, salt, and pepper. Arrange around the salmon on the sheet pan, cut-side up.

 Broil **8 to 10 minutes**. The salmon should be opaque, just flaking, and the miso glaze should be deeply caramelized and slightly charred. The bok choy stems should be tender-crisp and the leaves wilted with charred edges. Watch closely the last 2 minutes. Broilers vary wildly.

#### 5. Serve (2 min).

 Plate the rice. Add the salmon and bok choy. Drizzle the reserved miso glaze over the bok choy. Top with sliced scallions and sesame seeds. A lime wedge on the side.

### Nutrition per serving (approximate)

- Calories: ~580

- Protein: ~35 g

- Fiber: ~7 g

- Carbs: ~52 g (mostly complex from brown rice)

- Fat: ~22 g (mostly mono- and polyunsaturated)

- Omega-3 (EPA + DHA): ~1,500 mg

- Calcium: ~250 mg (bok choy + sesame seeds)

 The technique that matters most

 The broiler is the difference between a good miso salmon and a great one. The high direct heat caramelizes the sugar in the miso into a near-crust on top. The flavor people pay $34 for at a restaurant.

### Variations

- Different fish: Black cod (sablefish) is the classic. Fattier and more decadent. Halibut works too. Cooking time may shift slightly.

- Lower-carb: Skip the rice; double the bok choy and add sautéed shiitake mushrooms.

- Spicier: Add 1 tsp gochujang or 1/2 tsp chili crisp to the glaze.

- Meal-prep version: Cook everything Sunday, portion into 4 containers. The salmon reheats well in a 300°F oven for 6 minutes (not the microwave).
