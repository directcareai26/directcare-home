## One Week of Short Sleep and Testosterone

 In a 2011 University of Chicago study, a week of 5-hour nights lowered daytime testosterone by 10% to 15% in healthy young men.

 In a 2011 study published in JAMA, 10 healthy young men who were limited to 5 hours of sleep a night for one week had daytime testosterone levels 10% to 15% lower than when they were fully rested. For comparison, testosterone normally declines 1% to 2% a year with age.

 Most of the testosterone a man makes each day is released during sleep. Researchers at the University of Chicago tested what happens to daytime levels when that sleep is cut short for a week.

#### What the study did

 Ten healthy men, average age 24, stayed in a sleep laboratory for 11 days. They spent three nights with 10 hours in bed to become fully rested, followed by eight nights limited to 5 hours, from 12:30 a.m. to 5:30 a.m.

 Blood was drawn every 15 to 30 minutes across a full 24 hours, once at the end of the rested phase and once after a week of short nights.

#### What it found

- Daytime testosterone was 10% to 15% lower after the week of short sleep

- The drop was largest in the afternoon and evening, between 2 p.m. and 10 p.m.

- The men's self-rated vigor fell steadily across the week

 The authors put the size in context: normal aging lowers testosterone by 1% to 2% a year.

 Keep it in proportion

 This was a study of 10 young men in a lab for one week. It did not test older men, or whether levels recover after catch-up sleep. Lower levels on a blood test are also not the same as a diagnosis.

#### What this means for a blood test

 The Endocrine Society guideline says low testosterone should be diagnosed only in men with symptoms and consistently low levels, confirmed with a repeat morning fasting test. Poor sleep in the days before a test is one reason a single result can read low. #### Sleep habits worth fixing first

- Keep a regular bedtime and wake time

- Aim for seven or more hours a night

- Keep the bedroom dark, cool and quiet

- Limit alcohol in the evening, since it fragments sleep

#### When to talk with a clinician

 Loud snoring with pauses in breathing can indicate sleep apnea, which is linked with low testosterone and is treatable.
