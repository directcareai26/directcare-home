## Sleeping With a Light On: Heart Rate and Insulin Resistance

 In a 2022 lab study, one night of sleep in a moderately lit room raised overnight heart rate and next-morning insulin resistance in healthy adults.

 In a 2022 Northwestern University study, healthy adults who slept one night in moderate room light (100 lux) had a higher heart rate during sleep and higher insulin resistance the next morning than adults who slept in near darkness (under 3 lux).

 A television left on, a hallway light through an open door, a lamp you fell asleep under. Light in the bedroom at night is common, and most people assume closed eyes shut it out. A sleep laboratory at Northwestern University tested that assumption.

#### What the study did

 Healthy young adults spent two nights in the lab. One group slept both nights in dim light, under 3 lux, which is close to darkness. The other group slept the first night in dim light and the second night in moderate light of 100 lux. That is comparable to a dimly lit room.

 Researchers recorded sleep and heart activity through the night and tested how the body handled glucose the next morning.

#### What it found

 After one night in the lit room, compared with the dim room:

- Heart rate during sleep was higher

- Heart rate variability was lower, a sign the body's alert system stayed more active

- Insulin resistance the next morning was higher

 The participants were asleep with their eyes closed. Their bodies still registered the light.

 Keep it in proportion

 This was a small lab study of healthy adults over a single night. It shows the body reacts to light during sleep. It does not show that a night light causes diabetes or heart disease.

#### Why light would matter

 Eyelids block only part of the light that reaches them. The authors' explanation is that light during sleep keeps the nervous system in a more alert state, which raises heart rate, and that this state also makes cells respond less well to insulin.

#### How to darken a bedroom

- Turn the television off before sleep, or set a sleep timer

- Cover or unplug chargers and devices with bright indicator lights

- Use blackout curtains or a sleep mask if street light comes in

- If you need a night light for safety, choose a dim, warm-colored one and place it near the floor

 Sleep experts recommend seven or more hours a night for adults. Our article on [one night of short sleep and insulin sensitivity](https://www.directcare.ai/blog/one-night-short-sleep-insulin-sensitivity) covers what lost sleep does to the same system.

#### When to talk with a clinician

 Loud snoring, pauses in breathing, or daytime sleepiness that does not improve with more sleep are worth an assessment.
