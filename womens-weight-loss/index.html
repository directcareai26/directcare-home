Is compounded tirzepatide FDA-approved?
No. Compounded tirzepatide is not FDA-approved as a finished product. The active ingredient, tirzepatide, is individually FDA-approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration. Compounding is a regulated pharmacy practice (governed by USP 795/797 standards and state boards of pharmacy) used to produce personalized formulations when an FDA-approved finished product is not available or appropriate for a given patient. Compounded tirzepatide is not a generic of, and is not bioequivalent to, any FDA-approved branded tirzepatide product. Safety and efficacy of compounded preparations have not been independently verified by the FDA.
Is it the same as the branded GLP-1 medication?
It contains the same active molecule (tirzepatide) but it is a different product. Branded FDA-approved tirzepatide finished products have undergone separate FDA review for safety, efficacy, and manufacturing. Compounded tirzepatide is prepared by a US-licensed compounding pharmacy at clinician-specified strengths. The clinical evidence base for tirzepatide comes from studies of the FDA-approved branded product; compounded preparations have not been studied separately in randomized controlled trials.
What exactly is the Care Coaching, and who runs it?
Every DirectCare AI weight loss patient is paired with a personal Care Coach who is a Certified Medical Assistant specifically trained in GLP-1 protocols. Your first session is scheduled at signup and runs in the first 14 days, alongside your first 1 to 2 doses. After that, sessions happen every two weeks (or whatever cadence you prefer). Each session has three parts: (1) review of your treatment progress and side effects, (2) revisit your personal Care Plan and the goals you set, and (3) introduce a new coaching module, with an easy-to-digest summary you can revisit anytime in your member portal.
Is Care Coaching the same as a dietitian or therapist?
No. Care Coaching is medically-centered support for your weight loss treatment, provided by Certified Medical Assistants. It is not registered dietitian services, not a prescriptive diet or eating restrictions, not mental health therapy, and not a substitute for other specialized professional services. Coaching covers GLP-1 side effect management, nutrition strategies for shrinking appetite, sleep, stress, emotional eating patterns, and social situations. Everything is at your discretion. Our concierge team reaches out to offer support and help maintain your appointment rhythm, but you can pause, reschedule, change cadence, or skip sessions at any time. Your Care Plan stays with you, when you're ready to resume, you pick up where you left off.
Will tirzepatide work in perimenopause or postpartum?
The underlying mechanism — dual GLP-1 / GIP receptor activation — works regardless of hormonal stage. But perimenopause, postpartum, PCOS, and birth control all change how appetite, insulin, and water retention behave. Your DirectCare AI clinician adjusts titration based on how your body responds, and your Care Coach is specifically trained on hormonal weight gain, sleep disruption, and cravings unique to female metabolism. Tirzepatide is not appropriate while you are pregnant, planning pregnancy, or breastfeeding.
Is this safe with hormonal birth control?
GLP-1 medications can slow gastric emptying, which may temporarily reduce absorption of oral contraceptives — especially when starting or stepping up a dose. Most clinicians recommend using a backup non-hormonal method (e.g., barrier) for the first 4 weeks after starting and after any dose increase. IUDs, implants, and patches are not affected. Discuss your specific birth control with your DirectCare AI clinician during intake.
Who is not a candidate?
You should not start tirzepatide if you or a family member have a history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN 2), severe gastrointestinal disease (including gastroparesis), severe pancreatitis, severe kidney impairment, or if you are pregnant, planning pregnancy, or breastfeeding. The clinician will also evaluate BMI, medication interactions, eating-disorder history, and recent gallbladder disease. We turn down patients who aren't appropriate, at no charge.
What are the most common side effects?
Most patients experience some nausea, reflux, or constipation in the first 1 to 2 weeks of starting or stepping up a dose. Less common: vomiting, fatigue, injection-site reaction. Rare but serious: pancreatitis, gallbladder issues, allergic reaction. Your clinician will give you a side-effect protocol and an anti-nausea medication (Zofran) where indicated, and your Care Coach will run the side-effect-management module in your first sessions.
Do I need insurance? Can I use HSA / FSA?
No insurance is required, DirectCare AI is direct-pay. Most patients use HSA or FSA dollars; we can provide an itemized receipt with the appropriate codes. Cash, debit, and major credit cards accepted.
How is it shipped?
Compounded tirzepatide ships in plain, unmarked, temperature-controlled packaging from the US compounding pharmacy. You'll get tracking. Most orders arrive in 3 to 5 business days after clinician approval. Refills auto-ship, you can pause, adjust, or cancel anytime from your patient portal.
What if I want to stop?
You can pause or cancel any plan at any time. Stopping tirzepatide is straightforward, there's no withdrawal, but appetite typically returns to baseline within a few weeks, so weight regain is common without a maintenance strategy. Your Care Coach will help you build a step-down or maintenance plan when you're ready.
